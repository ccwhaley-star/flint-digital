How Much Should a Small Business Website Cost in 2026?

← Insights  ·  Flint Digital  ·  4 min read

Ask five agencies what a website costs and you'll get five answers spanning two orders of magnitude. Here's the honest breakdown — including our own number, because pricing you have to “book a call” to learn isn't pricing.

The market, roughly

  • DIY template builders: $200–$500/year forever, plus your evenings, plus the hidden costs — speed, rankings, sameness.
  • Freelancer template setups: $500–$2,000. Often a template with your logo swapped in. You inherit the platform's problems with an invoice attached.
  • Traditional agencies: $5,000–$50,000, in weeks-to-months, because you're paying for project managers, account reps, and revision meetings — not more website.
  • AI-first, hand-coded (us): $1,500 flat, delivered in days. AI handles the volume work; a human operator handles judgment. That's the entire trick — the savings are structural, not a discount.

What you should actually pay for

Not pages. Outcomes. A small business site earns its cost when it loads instantly, says clearly what you do and where, ranks for your service in your area, gets you cited by AI assistants, and turns visitors into calls. Those come from engineering and structure — schema, speed, answer-shaped content — not from a fourth round of homepage animations.

The recurring-cost trap

The quote is only half the question. Ask what it costs to keep: platform fees, plugin renewals, “maintenance” retainers with no defined output. Our model is month-to-month with no contracts — hosting and monitoring at $249, and growth plans at $649–$999 for businesses that want the rankings actively pushed. Cancel with an email. If a vendor needs a long contract to keep you, ask why the work can't.

One honest rule of thumb

If a website quote can't be explained in terms of what it will earn you — calls, bookings, customers — it's a decoration quote. Decoration is fine. It's just not what a small business needs from the internet.
