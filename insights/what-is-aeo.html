What is Answer Engine Optimization (AEO)?

Your customers are starting to ask AI assistants for recommendations instead of scrolling Google. AEO is how you become the business the AI names — and right now, most of your competitors haven't figured it out.

For twenty years, "getting found online" meant one thing: ranking on Google. You optimized pages, earned links, and tried to climb the blue list of results. That still matters. But a second front has opened, and it's moving fast.

People now ask ChatGPT "who's the best plumber in Olathe?" They ask Gemini "is this roofing company any good?" They read Google's AI Overview at the top of the page and never scroll to the links below. In each case, an AI gives one answer — or a short list. If your business isn't in it, you don't exist for that customer.

So what is AEO, exactly?

Answer Engine Optimization is the practice of making your business easy for AI systems to find, understand, and trust — so they recommend you when someone asks. Where SEO targets a ranking, AEO targets a citation: being the source the model pulls from and the name it says out loud.

Why it matters now

Three things are happening at once:

  • AI answers are replacing clicks. When the assistant answers directly, there's no page of ten options to compete on. There's one answer, and maybe a runner-up.
  • The models favor what they can verify. Clear, structured, consistent information across your site and the web makes you a safe recommendation. Gaps make you invisible.
  • Almost nobody is optimizing for it yet. That's the opening. The businesses that get this right now will be the defaults their competitors have to claw back later.

How to start

The fundamentals are unglamorous but they work:

  • Structured data. Mark up your business, services, location, and FAQs so machines read them unambiguously.
  • Consistent citations. Your name, address, phone, and hours should match everywhere they appear online.
  • Reviews. Real, recent reviews are one of the strongest trust signals an AI can see.
  • Plain, factual content. Answer the actual questions customers ask, in plain language the models can lift directly.
